What is the ticket fee comparison with other services.  


Here’s a side-by-side view of common ticketing platform fees compared to what you might charge with a DIY system like WeUP MVP. This puts the cost of third-party services in context so you can price competitively. 


Typical Ticketing Platform Fees (Organizer/Attendee Perspective)


Eventbrite – ~3.5–3.7% plus a flat per-ticket fee (commonly $1.59–$1.79 in the U.S.) plus payment processing (~2.9%). On a $50 ticket this can mean total fees (~$6.50+). 


Ticketmaster – Variable and often high: service fees can range ~10–20% or more of ticket face value (plus processing), especially for large concerts. 


Posh.vip (Posh) – ~10% plus a small flat fee (e.g., $0.99) charged to attendees. 


Yapsody – ~1.75% plus ~$0.50 per ticket (for basic general admission). 


Ticketbud – Roughly ~$0.99 + ~2% per ticket. 


Low-Fee/Niche Options (Cheapest seen)
Platforms like Hi.Events claim ~0.75% + ~$0.40 per ticket, making them among the lowest typical combinations mentioned publicly. 


Resale Marketplaces
Resale sites like StubHub may collect ~10–15% from buyer and/or seller on top of original ticket processing — but that’s secondary market, not primary ticket sales. 


How DIY (WeUP MVP) Compares
In your own system, your platform fee structure is entirely in your control. You’ll likely have:


Stripe Processing: ~2.9% + $0.30 per transaction (card processing cost)


Platform Fee: Your cut (example 5–10% or a flat fee)


SMS/Delivery: Twilio SMS cost per message (small fixed cost)


QR/Analytics: No incremental per-ticket fee from external providers


Hypothetical WeUP Fee Example (control vs cost):


Fee Type        Amount        Why it Matters
Stripe        ~2.9% + $0.30        Standard card processing
Platform Fee        5–8% or a flat $1–$2        Your revenue split
SMS        ~$0.01–$0.05/message        Delivery cost
Total ≈ 8–11% all-in — likely lower than Eventbrite’s combined fees and much lower than Ticketmaster for comparable events. You also get full control of branding, data, and payout timing, not just a marketplace platform fee.


What This Means for Organizers
Established platforms like Eventbrite are functional but expensive at scale — especially with flat fees stacking on top of percentage fees. 


Big venue ticketing (like Ticketmaster) can be a huge cut but offers massive distribution and inventory infrastructure behind the scenes. 


White-label or DIY systems (like your WeUP MVP) let you keep more revenue per ticket and customize pricing — but you must build and operate more of the stack yourself.


The differences are more pronounced at higher ticket prices because flat fees become a smaller percentage, while percentage-based fees remain proportional.

## 8) “Encounter” — The WeUP Shop as a discovery surface, not a catalog


**Purpose:** the shop should feel *encountered*—tied to the moment.


* **Shop is event-bound + time-bound:** live shop windows, residual shops, gated visibility. 
* **Layer 1:** tap a booth pin → “What’s live here” (3–6 items max).
* **Layer 2:** item story (why it exists *in this event*, what unlocks it: attendance, ritual, tier).

## 9) “Signal” — Light language control as a tiny, dignified panel

* **Layer 2:** choose mode (“observing,” “participating,” “ritual,” “private”), with a preview animation. 
* **Layer 3:** “Venue Rules Override” (some spaces cap intensity; some rituals enforce silence). 


**Non-negotiable:** light communicates **state**, not identity; “off” always means privacy/non-participation.

## 10) “Afterglow” — Post-event memory, receipts, and residual unlocks
